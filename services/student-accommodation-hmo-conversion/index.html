The rules that make or break an HMO scheme

The Article 4 direction is the first question. In many London boroughs (Barking and Dagenham, Barnet, Camden, Newham, Redbridge, Waltham Forest and others) an Article 4 direction has removed the permitted-development right to change use from C3 (dwellinghouse) to C4 (small HMO, up to 6 residents). Where Article 4 is in force, planning permission is required for the change of use, and the viability of the scheme has to account for planning risk, application fees and programme impact.

The Licensing of Houses in Multiple Occupation (Prescribed Description) (England) Order 2018 sets a mandatory licensing threshold at 5 or more residents from two or more households, and the HMO Management Regulations 2018 set enforceable minimum sleeping room sizes: 6.51 sqm for one person over ten, 4.64 sqm for a child under ten, 10.22 sqm for two people. Individual borough licences add further requirements: minimum kitchen and bathroom provision per person, insulation and ventilation standards, fire strategy per LACORS or BS 9991, and periodic inspections.

Frequently asked questions

What is Article 4 and how does it affect HMO conversion?

An Article 4 direction is a legal instrument that removes specified permitted-development rights in a defined area. Many London boroughs have Article 4 directions removing the permitted-development right to change use from Class C3 (dwellinghouse) to Class C4 (small HMO, 3 to 6 unrelated occupiers). Where Article 4 is in force, planning permission is required for the change of use, on top of any licensing. This affects viability: application fee, professional costs, planning risk and programme.

What are the minimum bedroom sizes for an HMO?

Under the Licensing of Houses in Multiple Occupation (Mandatory Conditions of Licences) (England) Regulations 2018, minimum sleeping room floor areas are: 6.51 sqm for one person aged over ten, 4.64 sqm for one child under ten, and 10.22 sqm for two people over ten sharing. Any room below 4.64 sqm cannot be used as sleeping accommodation. Individual boroughs may set higher standards in additional licensing schemes.

LACORS or BS 9991: which fire strategy applies?

For most converted HMOs up to three storeys, the LACORS Housing Fire Safety guidance (2008) is the recognised standard. For larger, more complex or purpose-built houses in multiple occupation, BS 9991 (fire safety in the design and management of residential buildings) is used. Both require interlinked mains-powered fire detection, protected escape routes, appropriate fire doors and compartmentation, and periodic inspection and testing.

How does the HMO licence application work?

The application is made to the borough where the property is located, with a plan showing room sizes and fire strategy, the applicant's fit-and-proper-person declaration, and the fee (varies by borough, typically £700 to £1,500 per property). The council inspects and either grants a licence for up to five years or requires works before grant. Additional or selective licensing schemes in some boroughs add further requirements even where mandatory licensing does not apply.

Do all boroughs operate selective licensing?

No. Selective licensing schemes apply to all private-rented properties in a defined ward or borough, and require a scheme designation and Secretary of State approval. Boroughs currently operating some form of additional or selective licensing include Newham, Waltham Forest, Croydon, Enfield and others, though schemes are refreshed periodically. Any acquisition is checked against the borough's current licensing designation.

What yield uplift can HMO conversion deliver?

HMO gross yields typically run at 8% to 14% depending on postcode, unit mix and operating cost, compared with 3% to 6% for the same property let on an AST to a single household. Net yield is compressed by higher void, higher management fees, higher maintenance and licence fees. Post-Article 4, the value uplift on the property is also affected because a future buyer without HMO planning cannot use it as one.
