What makes London extensions and lofts different

London terraces sit on tight sites with immediate neighbours on both sides. Almost every rear extension or loft conversion triggers the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 (Section 3 for the wall of separation, Section 2 for interference with the party structure, Section 6 for excavations near foundations). Getting notices served correctly, on the right neighbours, is a legal precondition to lawful work, not paperwork to be caught up on later.

Permitted development rights under the General Permitted Development Order 2015 (Class A for extensions, Class AA for storey-additions, Class B for loft conversions) sound generous but are heavily conditioned: no wrap-arounds under PD, size limits vary between article 2(3) land and the rest, and Article 4 directions in many London boroughs remove PD entirely. We check the exact rights on your property before pricing.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need planning permission for a rear extension?

Many single-storey rear extensions fall under Permitted Development (GPDO Class A) up to defined size limits, subject to a Prior Approval Neighbour Consultation for the larger PD sizes. A Lawful Development Certificate is strongly recommended even where PD applies, to document lawfulness before onward sale. In Article 4 areas, conservation areas and on listed buildings, PD is often removed and a full planning application is required.

What is a Party Wall Notice and when is one required?

The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 requires written notice to affected neighbours before works to a party wall (Section 2), building at the boundary (Section 1) or excavating near a neighbour's foundations (Section 6). Notices must be served with statutory notice periods (one or two months depending on the section). If a neighbour dissents or does not respond, party wall surveyors are appointed and an award is agreed before works start.

How long does a typical rear extension take?

A single-storey rear extension in a London terrace typically runs 14 to 22 weeks on site depending on size, foundation type, whether the kitchen is included and the finishes spec. A loft conversion is typically 10 to 16 weeks. Programme includes contingency for party wall award periods and inspection windows.

What are the fire-safety rules for a loft conversion?

Adding a third habitable storey to a two-storey house requires a protected stair (30-minute fire doors to all rooms opening onto the stair), mains-linked interlinked smoke alarms and a means-of-escape route to a final exit. The exact route (window escape, external escape, or protected internal escape) depends on the layout and is signed off by Building Control.

What is a build-over agreement?

A written agreement from the water authority (Thames Water in most of London) allowing construction over or within a set distance of a public sewer. Required before excavation and typically takes four to eight weeks to obtain. We include the application in the pre-construction programme.

What Building Control sign-off do I need?

A Full Plans submission or Building Notice under the Building Regulations 2010, with inspections at foundation, drainage, structural, insulation, MEP and completion stages. Sign-off is issued as the Completion Certificate on satisfactory final inspection, and is normally required by mortgage lenders and buyers.
