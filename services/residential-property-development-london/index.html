Where residential development succeeds or fails

Residential development succeeds when the unit mix, the specification and the programme are set against realistic comparable evidence at the acquisition stage. It fails when the sponsor is convinced their scheme will sell at a premium over local comparables, or when a marginal site is offered up as an affordable-housing exception it cannot bear. Rigorous underwriting at acquisition, not at Practical Completion, is the difference.

London-specific rules add to the challenge: the London Plan sets density and dual-aspect expectations, borough Local Plans set affordable-housing thresholds (35% or 50% depending on the borough and route), s.106 obligations and CIL apply, and any scheme over 10 units usually needs an affordable-housing viability assessment. Getting these on the table at pre-application is the difference between a 12-week determination and a two-year appeal.

Frequently asked questions

Do you own sites yourselves?

We do not run a permanent land bank, but we do co-invest on selected schemes as an equity partner in a special-purpose vehicle. Most sites are introduced to us by the sponsor, a joint-venture partner or a landowner looking for a delivery route to consent and construction.

What affordable housing percentage will a London scheme need?

The London Plan sets a strategic target of 50% affordable housing, with a threshold approach: schemes providing 35% affordable housing (or 50% on public land or industrial-release sites) under the Fast Track Route avoid a full viability assessment. Schemes below the threshold trigger a full viability assessment; each borough's Local Plan supplements this.

Is PRS or private for sale the better exit?

Depends on the scheme, the postcode and the sponsor's investment horizon. PRS suits longer holds, steady yield and a lower unit-mix concentration in high-value units. Private for sale suits shorter holds, higher IRR volatility and dual-aspect corner units at the top of the value bracket. We model both cases at underwriting.

What is the optimum unit mix?

The mix that maximises GDV per net internal square metre for the target buyer or tenant. Central London typically favours 1B2P and 2B3P units for private for sale; outer London and the Home Counties favour more 3B units and family-sized product. Any mix has to comply with the London Plan and the borough's Local Plan minimum internal space standards and dual-aspect requirements.

What structural warranty do buyers expect?

For new-build and substantially refurbished residential units: NHBC, LABC, BuildZone, ICW or CRL 10-year structural warranty. Mortgage lenders' panels vary; NHBC has the widest acceptance, but the alternatives are widely accepted where the fee saving offsets the acceptance risk. We confirm the buyer's likely lender panel at underwriting stage.

How do you handle post-completion snagging?

The rectification period runs for 12 months from Practical Completion under a standard JCT contract. Snagging identified at handover is logged, prioritised and cleared before completion of the sale where possible. Defects arising in the rectification period are attended on the same reactive SLA as our maintenance contracts.
