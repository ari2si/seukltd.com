What statutory compliance means in practice

Statutory compliance is where property owners take on personal liability, not just contractual liability. Failure to hold a current EICR (BS 7671), gas safety certificate, fire risk assessment, fire door inspection record, asbestos register or legionella risk assessment (HSG274 / ACOP L8) is enforceable by fine and, in a serious case, prosecution. A single central compliance register with dated evidence for every asset in the portfolio is the minimum defensible standard.

Since the Building Safety Act 2022 and the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022, additional obligations apply to higher-risk residential buildings: quarterly fire door inspections in common areas, annual door inspections to individual flats, PEEP considerations, and information provided to residents and to the local Fire and Rescue Service. Portfolios that do not distinguish those buildings from the general stock are the ones that miss the deadlines.

Frequently asked questions

What sits under statutory compliance?

The core statutory items for most non-domestic and multi-let residential properties are: Fixed Wire Testing (EICR) to BS 7671; annual gas safety inspection (Gas Safe); Fire Risk Assessment under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 and the Fire Safety Act 2021; fire door inspections per the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022; legionella risk assessment and monitoring per HSG274 / L8; asbestos duty to manage under CAR 2012; LOLER inspections on lifts every six months; PAT testing per HSE guidance.

How often should EICR be renewed?

Non-domestic properties: at intervals not exceeding five years, or on change of tenancy. Domestic PRS: at intervals not exceeding five years under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, and on change of tenant if flagged. HMOs: as required by the licence conditions, typically at least every five years. Any recommendation coded C1 or C2 in the EICR requires prompt remedial action.

What changed with fire door inspections after Grenfell?

The Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022, in force from January 2023, require the Responsible Person of a building over 11 metres containing two or more residential units to carry out quarterly checks of all fire doors in common parts and annual checks (on a best-endeavours basis) of flat entrance doors. Records must be kept and made available on request.

What is a realistic P1 reactive response target?

For a residential or commercial property with an on-call rota: 2 to 4 hours attendance in central London and up to 6 hours in outer boroughs and the Home Counties. Definitions of P1 (make safe / uninhabitable / significant loss) are written into the contract so both sides agree on what triggers the P1 clock.

What turnaround times are realistic for a void flat?

A cosmetic void with EICR renewal and gas safety re-check: 3 to 5 working days. A void requiring minor works (small kitchen or bathroom repairs, plaster repairs, redecoration): 7 to 12 working days. Larger refurbishments run to programme. Every void is logged with a target return-to-market date at the point the keys are received.

Data can be exported into the client's own CAFM (Elogbooks, Concept Evolution, MRI, Yardi) where the contract requires.
