What makes a London basement different

Basements below existing homes in London and the Home Counties are not a scaled-up extension. The site is tight, the neighbours are close, the ground is often clay over water-bearing sand, and every borough now has its own basement policy on depth, footprint under garden, lightwells and secondary storeys. Camden, Kensington and Chelsea, Westminster, Islington and Hammersmith and Fulham all publish restrictions that would refuse projects that were routine ten years ago.

The commercial cost of getting any of that wrong is out of proportion to the fee saving. A refused planning application, a party wall dispute after excavation has started, or a waterproofing detail that the CSSW designer would not have signed off, each adds months and six-figure sums. The point of a specialist basement team is that these decisions are made once, up front, by the people who will also carry the risk of building it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need planning permission for a basement in London?

Most single-storey basements under an existing footprint fall under permitted development, but every London borough now has additional basement policies. Camden, Kensington and Chelsea, Westminster, Islington and Hammersmith and Fulham all restrict depth, footprint under garden, lightwells and secondary storeys. We review your borough's policy at feasibility and tell you whether a full planning application is likely before any design fees are committed.

When does the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 apply?

Section 6 of the Act applies to any excavation within 3 metres of a neighbouring structure that goes deeper than the neighbour's foundations, and within 6 metres if the excavation line crosses a 45-degree plane from the base of those foundations. Almost every London basement triggers it. We coordinate with your appointed party wall surveyor, provide the drawings and method statements they need to serve notices, and programme temporary works around the award.

How long does a typical basement take?

A single-storey basement under an existing Victorian terrace typically takes 26 to 40 weeks from site set-up to practical completion, depending on ground conditions, waterproofing strategy and fit-out spec. A double-storey or garden-extended basement can run 40 to 60 weeks. We give a programme with critical-path milestones at tender stage, not a headline number.

What waterproofing approach do you use?

We design to BS 8102:2022, almost always as a combined Type A (barrier) and Type C (cavity drainage) system for habitable Grade 3 space, with a CSSW-qualified waterproofing designer named on the drawings. Sump and pump provision is duplicated with battery backup on any habitable basement. The waterproofing designer stays involved through construction so the as-built system matches the specified one.

How is the property monitored during excavation?

A temporary works engineer signs off every excavation and underpinning sequence. Movement monitoring is installed on the host structure and, where the party wall award requires it, on adjoining properties, with tell-tales and precise-level readings taken to an agreed frequency and shared with the party wall surveyors.

Do you provide a warranty?

Waterproofing warranties are provided by the specified system manufacturer, typically for 10 or 30 years. Independent structural warranties (BuildZone, LABC, ICW, CRL) are arranged where the basement is part of a new-build or major redevelopment. We scope the right cover with you against the intended use and any onward sale plans.
